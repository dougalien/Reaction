Where the data and models come from

Every number Reaction reports is computed from the published data and equations below. Links go to the publisher (DOI) or to the official download where one exists. The thermodynamic databases are not part of the app: they are read from your data folder, where scripts/fetch_references.ps1 puts them.

Thermodynamic databases

  • OBIGT, the database of CHNOSZ: Dick, J.M. (2019) CHNOSZ: Thermodynamic calculations and diagrams for geochemistry. Frontiers in Earth Science 7, 180. doi:10.3389/feart.2019.00180; data files from github.com/jedick/CHNOSZ (GPL-3.0). Gibbs energies and HKF and Maier-Kelley parameters of aqueous species, minerals and gases (SUPCRT92, SLOP98, the inorganic and organic files).
  • Johnson, J.W., Oelkers, E.H. and Helgeson, H.C. (1992) SUPCRT92. Computers & Geosciences 18, 899–947. doi:10.1016/0098-3004(92)90029-Q The SUPCRT92 dataset inside OBIGT and its scaling conventions.
  • Helgeson, H.C., Delany, J.M., Nesbitt, H.W. and Bird, D.K. (1978) Summary and critique of the thermodynamic properties of rock-forming minerals. American Journal of Science 278-A, 1–229. The older mineral data, used when the Berman files are missing.
  • Berman, R.G. (1988) Internally-consistent thermodynamic data for minerals in the system Na2O-K2O-CaO-MgO-FeO-Fe2O3-Al2O3-SiO2-TiO2-H2O-CO2. Journal of Petrology 29, 445–522. doi:10.1093/petrology/29.2.445 Silicate minerals, with the later additions in CHNOSZ extdata/Berman.
  • EQ3/6 version 8.0a (Wolery, T.J., Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory), the data0 files: github.com/LLNL/EQ3_6. Ion sizes, the Drummond CO2 coefficients, ideal solid solutions and the speciation test cases.
  • Wagman, D.D. and others (1982) The NBS tables of chemical thermodynamic properties. Journal of Physical and Chemical Reference Data 11, Supplement 2. Element entropies that put Berman energies on the OBIGT scale.

Equations of state and water

  • Helgeson, H.C., Kirkham, D.H. and Flowers, G.C. (1981) American Journal of Science 281, 1249–1516. doi:10.2475/ajs.281.10.1249
  • Tanger, J.C. and Helgeson, H.C. (1988) Revised equations of state for the standard partial molal properties of ions and electrolytes. American Journal of Science 288, 19–98. doi:10.2475/ajs.288.1.19
  • Shock, E.L., Oelkers, E.H., Johnson, J.W., Sverjensky, D.A. and Helgeson, H.C. (1992) Journal of the Chemical Society, Faraday Transactions 88, 803–826. doi:10.1039/FT9928800803 The g function of the revised HKF equations.
  • Helgeson, H.C. and Kirkham, D.H. (1974) American Journal of Science 274, 1089–1198. doi:10.2475/ajs.274.10.1089 Debye-Hückel parameters of water.
  • Wagner, W. and Pruss, A. (2002) The IAPWS formulation 1995 for the thermodynamic properties of ordinary water substance. Journal of Physical and Chemical Reference Data 31, 387–535. doi:10.1063/1.1461829
  • IAPWS-IF97, region 4 (vapour pressure of water): iapws.org.
  • Johnson, J.W. and Norton, D. (1991) Critical phenomena in hydrothermal systems. American Journal of Science 291, 541–648. doi:10.2475/ajs.291.6.541 Dielectric constant of water.
  • Marshall, W.L. and Franck, E.U. (1981) Ion product of water substance, 0–1000 °C, 1–10,000 bars. Journal of Physical and Chemical Reference Data 10, 295–304. doi:10.1063/1.555643 The reliability rating shown before each run and under the plot.
  • Peng, D.-Y. and Robinson, D.B. (1976) A new two-constant equation of state. Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Fundamentals 15, 59–64. doi:10.1021/i160057a011 Fugacity coefficients of the gas phase; critical constants from Poling, Prausnitz and O'Connell (2001) The Properties of Gases and Liquids, 5th ed., McGraw-Hill.

Activity models

  • Helgeson, H.C. (1969) Thermodynamics of hydrothermal systems at elevated temperatures and pressures. American Journal of Science 267, 729–804. doi:10.2475/ajs.267.7.729 The B-dot equation (default activity model), tabulated to 300 °C.
  • Davies, C.W. (1962) Ion Association. Butterworths, London. The Davies equation, with the 0.2 coefficient EQ3/6 uses.
  • Drummond, S.E. (1981) Boiling and mixing of hydrothermal fluids: chemical effects on mineral precipitation. PhD thesis, Pennsylvania State University. Salting-out of CO2, O2, H2 and other neutral species.
  • Harvie, C.E., Møller, N. and Weare, J.H. (1984) The prediction of mineral solubilities in natural waters. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 48, 723–751. doi:10.1016/0016-7037(84)90098-X The Pitzer equations for brines.
  • Pitzer, K.S. (1975) Thermodynamics of electrolytes. V. Effects of higher-order electrostatic terms. Journal of Solution Chemistry 4, 249–265. doi:10.1007/BF00646562 Unsymmetrical mixing (E-theta).
  • Parkhurst, D.L. and Appelo, C.A.J. (2013) Description of input and examples for PHREEQC version 3. U.S. Geological Survey Techniques and Methods 6-A43. doi:10.3133/tm6A43; PHREEQC download. The Pitzer parameters (pitzer.dat), the exchange and surface constants (phreeqc.dat) and the rate table (phreeqc_rates.dat).
  • Appelo, C.A.J., Parkhurst, D.L. and Post, V.E.A. (2014) Equations for calculating hydrogeochemical reactions of minerals and gases such as CO2 at high pressures and temperatures. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 125, 49–67. doi:10.1016/j.gca.2013.10.003
  • Appelo, C.A.J. (2015) Principles, caveats and improvements in databases for calculating hydrogeochemical reactions in saline waters from 0 to 200 °C and 1 to 1000 atm. Applied Geochemistry 55, 62–71. Temperature terms of the Pitzer parameters.

Kinetics

  • Palandri, J.L. and Kharaka, Y.K. (2004) A compilation of rate parameters of water-mineral interaction kinetics for application to geochemical modeling. U.S. Geological Survey Open-File Report 2004-1068. doi:10.3133/ofr20041068 Dissolution rate constants and activation energies of about 60 minerals.
  • Lasaga, A.C. (1984) Chemical kinetics of water-rock interactions. Journal of Geophysical Research 89, 4009–4025. doi:10.1029/JB089iB06p04009 The form of the rate law.
  • Gislason, S.R. and Oelkers, E.H. (2003) Mechanism, rates, and consequences of basaltic glass dissolution. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 67, 3817–3832. doi:10.1016/S0016-7037(03)00176-5 Basalt glass dissolution rate.

Catabolic energy (chemosynthesis)

  • McCollom, T.M. and Shock, E.L. (1997) Geochemical constraints on chemolithoautotrophic metabolism by microorganisms in seafloor hydrothermal systems. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 61, 4375–4391. doi:10.1016/S0016-7037(97)00241-X The method: vent fluid mixed into seawater with redox reactions held back, and the energy of each reaction per kg of fluid from ΔG and the limiting reactant.
  • Amend, J.P., McCollom, T.M., Hentscher, M. and Bach, W. (2011) Catabolic and anabolic energy for chemolithoautotrophs in deep-sea hydrothermal systems hosted in different rock types. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 75, 5736–5748. doi:10.1016/j.gca.2011.07.041 The set of aerobic and anaerobic reactions, and energy per kg of mixed fluid.

Isotope fractionation

  • Vho, A., Lanari, P. and Rubatto, D. (2020) An internally-consistent database for oxygen isotope fractionation between minerals. Journal of Petrology 60, 2101–2129. doi:10.1093/petrology/egaa001 (CC BY 4.0) Mineral-water oxygen fractionation.
  • Ohmoto, H. and Rye, R.O. (1979) Isotopes of sulfur and carbon. In Barnes, H.L. (ed.) Geochemistry of Hydrothermal Ore Deposits, 2nd ed., Wiley, 509–567. Carbon (against CO2) and sulfur (against H2S) fractionation.
  • Ohmoto, H. and Lasaga, A.C. (1982) Kinetics of reactions between aqueous sulfates and sulfides in hydrothermal systems. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 46, 1727–1745. doi:10.1016/0016-7037(82)90113-2 Sulfate-sulfide fractionation.
  • Horita, J. and Wesolowski, D.J. (1994) Liquid-vapor fractionation of oxygen and hydrogen isotopes of water from the freezing to the critical temperature. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 58, 3425–3437. doi:10.1016/0016-7037(94)90096-5 δ18O and δD of water through evaporation and boiling.
  • Suzuoki, T. and Epstein, S. (1976) Hydrogen isotope fractionation between OH-bearing minerals and water. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 40, 1229–1240. doi:10.1016/0016-7037(76)90158-7 Muscovite and the general mineral-water D/H equation.
  • Graham, C.M., Harmon, R.S. and Sheppard, S.M.F. (1984) Experimental hydrogen isotope studies: hydrogen isotope exchange between amphibole and water. American Mineralogist 69, 128–138. minsocam.org (PDF) Amphibole-water D/H.
  • Sheppard, S.M.F. and Gilg, H.A. (1996) Stable isotope geochemistry of clay minerals. Clay Minerals 31, 1–24. doi:10.1180/claymin.1996.031.1.01 Kaolinite-water D/H.
  • Chacko, T., Cole, D.R. and Horita, J. (2001) Equilibrium oxygen, hydrogen and carbon isotope fractionation factors applicable to geologic systems. Reviews in Mineralogy and Geochemistry 43, 1–81. doi:10.2138/gsrmg.43.1.1 The uncertainty quoted for mineral δD.

Brine and vapour (NaCl-H2O phase separation)

  • Driesner, T. (2007) The system H2O-NaCl. Part II: Correlations for molar volume, enthalpy, and isobaric heat capacity from 0 to 1000 °C, 1 to 5000 bar, and 0 to 1 XNaCl. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 71, 4902–4919. doi:10.1016/j.gca.2007.05.026
  • Oguntola, M.B. (2026) Thermodynamic lookup tables for saltwater systems based on Driesner & Heinrich (2007) correlations. Zenodo. doi:10.5281/zenodo.20394023 (CC BY 4.0) The table of coexisting brine and vapour (computed with xThermal).
  • Bischoff, J.L. and Rosenbauer, R.J. (1988) Liquid-vapor relations in the critical region of the system NaCl-H2O from 380 to 415 °C. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 52, 2121–2126. doi:10.1016/0016-7037(88)90192-5 The seawater two-phase boundary the app is checked against.

Starting fluids and rocks

  • Seawater: the EQ3/6 test case swmaj (EQ3/6 v8.0a, above).
  • Vent fluid: rounded after Von Damm, K.L., Edmond, J.M., Grant, B., Measures, C.I., Walden, B. and Weiss, R.F. (1985) Chemistry of submarine hydrothermal solutions at 21 °N, East Pacific Rise. Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 49, 2197–2220. doi:10.1016/0016-7037(85)90222-4
  • River water: Livingstone, D.A. (1963) Chemical composition of rivers and lakes. U.S. Geological Survey Professional Paper 440-G. doi:10.3133/pp440G
  • Great Salt Lake brine: Drever, J.I. (1997) The Geochemistry of Natural Waters, 3rd ed., Prentice Hall; and Jones, B.F., Naftz, D.L., Spencer, R.J. and Oviatt, C.G. (2009) Geochemical evolution of Great Salt Lake, Utah, USA. Aquatic Geochemistry 15, 123–195. doi:10.1007/s10498-008-9047-y
  • Basalt glass: close to the global mean of Gale, A., Dalton, C.A., Langmuir, C.H., Su, Y. and Schilling, J.-G. (2013) The mean composition of ocean ridge basalts. Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems 14, 489–518. doi:10.1029/2012GC004334
  • Deep seawater: the major ions of the seawater above, with dissolved O2, carbonate and pH rounded to values typical of deep Pacific water; illustrative, not a sample.
  • Rainwater and the contaminated stream are illustrative values, not samples.
